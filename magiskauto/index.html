MagiskAuto

Root your Google Pixel 6a or Samsung Galaxy Note 10 with Magisk. It shows you where to download your phone's exact firmware, then extracts and patches it for you.

Download the MagiskAuto package

Everything in one zip: MagiskAuto.exe, the Magisk app and a quick-start guide

Version 1.7.0 · 18 MB · Windows 10/11 (64-bit) · All releases

⚠️ Before you start

  • Your phone's bootloader must be unlocked first, and unlocking erases everything on the phone. Back up first.
  • US and Canada Note 10 models (SM-N970U / U1 / W) can't be unlocked, so they can't be rooted. The international Exynos SM-N970F works.
  • Rooting can void your warranty and stop some apps (such as banking apps) from working. You use this at your own risk.

How to use it

  1. Download the MagiskAuto package above and unzip it into a normal folder, such as Desktop or Downloads. Keep MagiskAuto.exe and its _internal folder together.
  2. On the phone, turn on USB debugging: Settings → About phone, tap Build number 7 times, then Developer options → USB debugging. Plug the phone into the PC and allow the connection.
  3. Double-click MagiskAuto.exe and choose your phone. The first time, it gets your PC ready: it sets up adb and fastboot (Google's official platform-tools; just press Enter), installs the Google USB Driver for the Pixel, and installs the Samsung USB Driver (from Samsung) for the Note 10. Then it shows the link to the right firmware for your phone.
  4. When the firmware has downloaded, drag the file into the MagiskAuto window and press Enter. It does the rest. The file can be in any folder, and you don't need to unzip it.
  5. Your results are saved in a magisk-output folder next to MagiskAuto.exe, or in Documents\MagiskAuto if it can't save there. You need about 12 GB free for the Note 10, or 4 GB for the Pixel.

Note 10 only: flashing needs Odin, Samsung's flashing tool. Samsung doesn't publish it, so MagiskAuto doesn't download it automatically. Save an Odin zip (e.g. Odin3 v3.14.4 from XDA Developers) to your Downloads folder. MagiskAuto finds it and does the flashing for you: it puts the phone in download mode, loads the files into Odin, clicks Start and waits for PASS.

If Windows doesn't see the phone, install the Google USB Driver (Pixel) or the Samsung USB Driver (Note 10).

Windows may show a "Windows protected your PC" warning because the app isn't code-signed. Click More info → Run anyway.

If your browser says "Virus detected", it's a false alarm caused by the app not being code-signed. Open Windows Security → Virus & threat protection → Protection history, choose the MagiskAuto entry, then Actions → Allow on device, and download it again.

Google Pixel 6a

  • Firmware: Google factory images (the factory image for your build, not the OTA)
  • Patches boot.img
  • Can flash it for you automatically
  • Keeps a copy of the stock image so you can undo it

Samsung Galaxy Note 10

  • Firmware: SamFw, SamMobile or Bifrost
  • Patches the AP file for Odin
  • Flashes it with Odin for you (download mode, loading the files, Start)
  • Afterwards, start the phone with Vol Up + Power (let go at the warning) to boot with root

Stuck on "Waiting for fastboot"?

This almost always means Windows has no driver for the phone in fastboot mode. MagiskAuto offers to install the Google USB Driver for you. To do it by hand: with the phone on the "Fastboot Mode" screen, open Device Manager and right-click Android (it has a yellow warning sign). Choose Update driver → Browse my computer and pick the usb_driver folder. Also try another cable or USB port.

Where does it get Magisk?

You don't need to download or point to Magisk. MagiskAuto uses the Magisk app already on your phone, or a Magisk .apk in the phone's Download folder or next to the exe. Otherwise it fetches the latest Magisk from GitHub.

Already have Magisk?

  • To update Magisk itself, you don't need this program: open the Magisk app → Install → Direct Install.
  • Lost root after a system update? Run MagiskAuto again with the firmware for the new build. It detects your Magisk and asks before re-patching.
  • Note 10: when re-patching, use HOME_CSC in Odin to keep your data. MagiskAuto puts it in the output folder.
